NORMALITY TEST & JUSTIFICATION — per-epoch ML_Over_ENMO (Mann-Whitney U)
========================================================================

Figure walksway_ML_Over_ENMO_epoch_bars.png compares POMS vs Healthy
ML_Over_ENMO within each of 6 one-minute epochs of the clinic 6MWT, using a
two-sided Mann-Whitney U test per epoch (BH-FDR corrected across the 6 epochs).

WHY NOT A t-TEST? The independent-samples t-test assumes each group is
approximately normal. We test this with Shapiro-Wilk within each cohort at each
epoch (H0: normal). Shapiro-Wilk p < 0.05 => normality rejected => the rank-based
Mann-Whitney U is the appropriate test. Skewness and excess kurtosis are listed
as supporting evidence (normal: skew=0, excess kurtosis=0).

CONCLUSION
------------------------------------------------------------------------
11 of 12 cohort-by-epoch distributions significantly depart from
normality (Shapiro-Wilk p < 0.05). ML_Over_ENMO is a ratio bounded at 0 and is
right-skewed in most epochs (positive skew, frequent positive excess kurtosis),
driven by a tail of high-sway subjects. The parametric t-test's normality
assumption is therefore not met across epochs. We use the two-sided Mann-Whitney
U test, which is non-parametric (rank-based), makes no normality assumption, and
is robust to skew and outliers; bars show medians with 95% bootstrap CIs (the
location/spread summaries consistent with a rank-based test), and per-epoch
p-values are BH-FDR corrected across the 6 epochs.

Tests: scipy.stats.shapiro ; scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu(alternative='two-sided')
skew/kurtosis: scipy.stats (Fisher, excess).
