JUSTIFICATION FOR USING THE MANN-WHITNEY U TEST
================================================================

Cohort comparison: POMS (n=50) vs Healthy (n=70), clinic 6MWT WalkSway
features. The figure walksway_significant_violins.png reports a two-sided
Mann-Whitney U (Wilcoxon rank-sum) test for each feature, with
Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction over the 14-feature WalkSway family.

WHY NOT A t-TEST?
----------------------------------------------------------------
The independent-samples t-test assumes each group is approximately
normally distributed. We tested this with the Shapiro-Wilk test within
each cohort (H0: data are normally distributed). A Shapiro-Wilk p < 0.05
means normality is rejected, violating the t-test assumption and making
the rank-based, distribution-free Mann-Whitney U test the appropriate
choice. Skewness and excess kurtosis are reported as supporting evidence
(a normal distribution has skew = 0 and excess kurtosis = 0).

CONCLUSION
----------------------------------------------------------------
At least one cohort distribution per feature significantly departs from
normality (Shapiro-Wilk p < 0.05), and the features are right-skewed
ratios bounded at 0. The normality assumption of the parametric t-test
is therefore not met. We use the two-sided Mann-Whitney U test, which is
non-parametric (rank-based), makes no normality assumption, and is robust
to skew and outliers. Group differences are summarized with medians and
95% bootstrap confidence intervals (consistent with a rank-based test),
and p-values are BH-FDR corrected across the WalkSway feature family.

Test: scipy.stats.mannwhitneyu(..., alternative='two-sided')
Normality: scipy.stats.shapiro ; skew/kurtosis: scipy.stats (Fisher, excess)
